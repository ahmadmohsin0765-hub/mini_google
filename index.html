How ARCHON Ranks Web Documents

In the early World Wide Web (1995-1998), first-generation search engines evaluated pages solely by counting how many times a keyword appeared. This made them vulnerable to keyword stuffing and spam.

ARCHON '98 combines 6 distinct signals into a composite score (0 to 100), simulating the revolutionary multi-signal algorithms that transformed web search in 1998.

1. Query Relevance (Title, Content & Topics)

Evaluates token presence across page fields. Titles receive a 4x multiplier, human-curated topics receive 2.5x, and descriptions receive 1.8x. Verbatim exact phrases receive a 25% bonus, while pages missing query terms face a coverage penalty.

2. Popularity (Log-Scaled View Count)

Measures aggregate readership. Crucially, raw views are scaled using a logarithmic curve: log10(views). This ensures a viral page with 400,000 views does not completely drown out an authoritative academic paper with 10,000 views.

Safeguard: Logarithmic dampening prevents viral skew

3. Reader Engagement (Dwell Time)

Tracks average reading duration. Rather than assuming "longer is always better", ARCHON models human reading behavior: rapid visits under 20 seconds are penalized as bounces, while 75 to 300 seconds represents optimal deep engagement. For extreme times (>10 minutes), an idle tab safeguard gently reduces points to prevent abandoned browser windows from gaming the system.

Safeguard: Non-linear plateau prevents idle tab domination

4. Hyperlink Citation Graph (Inbound & Outbound Links)

Inspired by academic citations and early PageRank research. Documents cited by reputable educational and standards institutions (.edu, .org, W3C) gain high citation authority. Pages linking out to link farms are penalized.

5. Aggregate User Behavior (CTR & Satisfaction)

Evaluates collective feedback: click-through rate (CTR) and post-click satisfaction rate (readers who found what they needed without immediately clicking Back). Uses aggregate demo data only; zero personal profiling.

6. Quality & Freshness

Assesses editorial baseline and timestamp recency, favoring clean structure and up-to-date documentation.
